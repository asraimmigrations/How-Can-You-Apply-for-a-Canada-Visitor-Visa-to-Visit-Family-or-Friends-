# How Can You Apply for a Canada Visitor Visa to Visit Family or Friends

For many people, visiting family members or friends in Canada is more than a holiday. It may be an opportunity to meet a new grandchild, attend a wedding, celebrate an important family occasion, or spend valuable time with loved ones who live far away.

However, before booking flights or making travel plans, visitors may need to obtain a Canadian visitor visa. Understanding the application process, preparing the right documents, and explaining the purpose of the visit clearly can help applicants submit a more complete application.

Whether you are planning to visit relatives in Montreal, spend time with friends in Toronto, or join family members in another Canadian city, this guide explains how to apply for a Canada visitor visa and how **Asra Immigration** can help you understand the process.

## What Is a Canada Visitor Visa?

A Canada visitor visa, officially known as a Temporary Resident Visa (TRV), is an immigration document that allows eligible foreign nationals to travel to a Canadian port of entry and request permission to enter Canada for a temporary stay.

People commonly apply for a visitor visa to:

- Visit parents, children, siblings, or other relatives.
- Spend time with friends living in Canada.
- Attend family celebrations, weddings, or anniversaries.
- Meet a newborn child or grandchild.
- Enjoy a holiday while visiting people they know.
- Attend certain personal or family events.

Not everyone needs a visitor visa. Depending on your citizenship, travel document, and circumstances, you may instead need an electronic travel authorization (eTA) or another document. Check the official Canadian government guidance before applying.

A visitor visa does not automatically guarantee entry into Canada. A border services officer makes the final decision when you arrive.

For current eligibility information, visit the Government of Canada's [visitor visa eligibility page](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/visit-canada/eligibility.html).

## Who Can Apply for a Visitor Visa to Visit Family or Friends?

You may be eligible to apply if you plan to visit Canada temporarily and meet the applicable immigration requirements.

Generally, you must demonstrate that:

- You have a valid passport or accepted travel document.
- You have sufficient money for your planned visit, or appropriate support from someone helping to pay for it.
- You have a genuine reason for travelling to Canada.
- You will leave Canada at the end of your authorized stay.
- You have ties to your home country or country of residence, where relevant.
- You meet applicable health, security, and admissibility requirements.

Your circumstances matter. For example, a retired parent visiting an adult child may provide different supporting documents from a salaried employee visiting a friend for two weeks.

Having a close relative in Canada does not automatically mean that a visa will be approved. The officer must assess the complete application and determine whether the applicant meets the requirements.

## Step-by-Step Guide to Applying for a Canada Visitor Visa

### Step 1: Confirm Which Travel Document You Need

Before starting an application, determine whether you need a visitor visa or an eTA.

Your nationality, passport, residence situation, and method of travel can affect the document you require. Do not assume that living in a particular country determines your visa requirements.

If you need a visitor visa, follow the instructions provided by Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) for applicants travelling to visit family or friends.

You can start with the official [How to Apply for a Visitor Visa guide](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/visit-canada/apply-visitor-visa.html).

### Step 2: Define the Purpose and Duration of Your Visit

Before preparing your paperwork, decide why you want to visit and approximately how long you intend to stay.

For example, your purpose might be to:

- Visit your daughter and son-in-law in Canada for three weeks.
- Attend your sister's wedding.
- Meet your new grandchild.
- Spend a holiday with a close friend.
- Visit family during a school holiday or special celebration.

Your proposed travel dates should be realistic and consistent with your circumstances. If you are employed, consider your approved leave dates. If you are retired, explain your intended travel period and personal circumstances clearly.

You should also decide where you will stay. If your relative will host you, include the relevant accommodation details in your supporting documents.

A clear travel purpose helps the officer understand the reason for your application.

### Step 3: Obtain an Invitation Letter From Your Host in Canada

An invitation letter is often a useful supporting document when someone applies to visit family or friends. The person inviting you may be a Canadian citizen, permanent resident, or temporary resident, depending on the circumstances.

The letter should explain the purpose of the visit and provide accurate information about both the visitor and the host.

A useful invitation letter generally includes:

- The visitor's full name and date of birth.
- The relationship between the visitor and the host.
- The purpose of the visit.
- The intended travel dates and approximate length of stay.
- The host's full name and contact information.
- The host's address in Canada.
- Details about accommodation arrangements.
- Information about who will pay for travel and living expenses.
- The host's immigration status in Canada, where relevant.

The host may also provide supporting documents, such as proof of status in Canada or evidence of their address, when appropriate.

**Important:** An invitation letter does not guarantee visa approval. It supports the application but does not replace the visitor's responsibility to meet the visa requirements.

For further details, consult the Government of Canada's guidance on [helping a family member or friend visit Canada](https://ircc.canada.ca/english/helpcentre/answer.asp?qnum=421).

### Step 4: Gather the Required Supporting Documents

Document preparation is one of the most important parts of a visitor visa application. The exact checklist depends on your situation, your country of application, and the instructions provided by IRCC.

Commonly useful documents include the following.

**1. Valid passport**

Provide a clear, colour copy of your valid passport or accepted travel document, including the pages required by the application instructions. Check its expiry date before applying.

**2. Proof of relationship**

If you are visiting a relative, documents such as birth certificates, marriage certificates, or other official records may help establish your relationship.

For instance, a person visiting an adult child may provide documents connecting the applicant to that child. The appropriate evidence depends on the family relationship and the available records.

**3. Invitation letter**

Include the letter from your host if applicable to your application. Make sure the information matches the details in your application form.

**4. Financial evidence**

You may need to show that you can pay for your visit. Depending on your circumstances, useful evidence may include bank statements, income records, pension statements, or other documents demonstrating your financial position.

If your host will cover some or all expenses, explain this clearly and include appropriate supporting evidence where requested.

**5. Employment or retirement documents**

An employer's letter, approved leave confirmation, business records, pension information, or other relevant evidence may help explain your circumstances and commitments outside Canada.

**6. Travel plans**

An itinerary, intended travel dates, accommodation details, and a brief explanation of planned activities can help establish the purpose and expected duration of your visit.

Avoid making non-refundable travel commitments before you understand the application process and the risks involved.

**7. Travel history**

Copies of previous visas, passports, and entry or exit stamps may be useful when requested or when they help explain your travel history.

**8. Additional forms and documents**

Depending on your situation, IRCC may require family information forms, explanations of specific circumstances, or additional documents identified in your personalized checklist.

Do not submit documents simply to make the application longer. Focus on evidence that is relevant, accurate, and consistent.

### Step 5: Complete the Online Application

Most visitor visa applications must be submitted online through the appropriate IRCC application system, subject to limited exceptions.

During the application process, you will provide personal information, passport details, travel plans, family information where required, and details about your visit.

When completing the application:

- Use the same spelling of your name as shown on your passport.
- Enter accurate travel dates and personal details.
- Explain your relationship with the person you plan to visit.
- Answer every question honestly.
- Check that the information agrees with your supporting documents.
- Upload documents in the required format.
- Review the application before submitting it.

If you are uncertain about a question, consult the official instructions rather than guessing.

The appropriate online portal will provide instructions about the forms and supporting documents needed for your application. Some applicants may need to use a different IRCC account or application route depending on their circumstances.

### Step 6: Pay the Applicable Application Fees

You must pay the applicable government fees when instructed during the application process.

According to the Government of Canada's visitor visa guidance, the standard visitor visa processing fee is currently listed as **CAD $100 per person**. Biometrics, if required, generally cost **CAD $85 per person**, subject to applicable exemptions and family fee limits.

These amounts can change. Check the official [visitor visa application instructions](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/visit-canada/apply-visitor-visa.html) for the latest fees before paying.

You should also budget for other possible expenses, including:

- Passport renewal, if necessary.
- Travel to a biometrics collection location.
- Document translation, where required.
- Medical examinations, if requested.
- Professional immigration assistance, if you choose to use it.

A complete budget helps you prepare without unexpected expenses.

### Step 7: Provide Biometrics if Required

Some applicants must provide biometrics, usually fingerprints and a photograph, as part of their application.

If biometrics are required, IRCC will provide instructions explaining what to do next. Wait for the relevant instruction letter and follow the directions to arrange an appointment at an authorized collection location.

Do not assume that you must provide biometrics again if you have provided them previously. Whether existing biometrics can be used depends on the applicable rules and your circumstances.

Respond promptly to any official request to help avoid unnecessary delays.

### Step 8: Monitor Your Application and Respond to Requests

After submitting your application, check your IRCC account and email regularly for updates.

IRCC may request additional documents, further information, a medical examination, or other steps depending on your circumstances. If you receive a request, read it carefully and respond within the stated deadline.

Processing times vary according to factors such as the applicant's location, application volume, and the complexity of the case. There is no single processing time that applies to every visitor visa application.

You can check the Government of Canada's [official processing-time tool](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/application/check-processing-times.html) for current estimates.

### Step 9: Follow the Instructions After a Decision

If your application is approved, follow the instructions provided by IRCC. Depending on your application, you may need to submit your passport or complete another step before the visa is issued.

Check the visa details carefully when you receive it. Confirm that your passport and travel documents are valid for your intended journey.

If your application is refused, read the refusal letter carefully to understand the officer's reasons. Consider whether additional evidence or a clearer explanation could address the concerns before deciding whether to submit another application.

Submitting a new application without addressing the reasons for refusal may not improve the outcome.

## What Should You Include in Your Invitation Letter?

A well-prepared invitation letter should be specific, truthful, and consistent with the visitor's application.

Here is a simple outline your host can adapt:

**Subject: Invitation for a Family or Friend to Visit Canada**

To whom it may concern,

I am writing to invite [visitor's full name], who is my [relationship], to visit me in Canada.

I currently live at [full Canadian address]. I would like to invite [visitor's name] to visit from approximately [proposed arrival date] to [proposed departure date] for the purpose of [brief explanation of the visit].

During the visit, [visitor's name] plans to stay at [accommodation address or arrangement]. The expenses for the visit will be covered by [visitor, host, or shared arrangement, as applicable].

I have provided my contact details and relevant supporting information for reference. Please contact me if further information is required.

Sincerely,

[Host's full name]  
[Address in Canada]  
[Telephone number]  
[Email address]

This is only a general example, not an official IRCC form. The host should adapt it to the actual circumstances and provide any additional details required by the official guidance.

## How Can You Demonstrate That You Will Return Home?

One of the key issues in a visitor visa application is whether the applicant will leave Canada at the end of the authorized stay.

An applicant should explain their personal circumstances honestly and provide relevant evidence where available.

Examples may include:

- **Employment:** A stable job, approved leave, or a letter confirming the expected return to work.
- **Business responsibilities:** Evidence of an active business and responsibilities that require the applicant's return.
- **Family responsibilities:** Relevant commitments involving family members who remain in the applicant's home country.
- **Property or financial commitments:** Documents demonstrating ongoing responsibilities or assets.
- **Studies:** Confirmation of enrolment or a return-to-study date.
- **Retirement arrangements:** Pension records, residence details, or other evidence of established circumstances.

Not every applicant will have every type of document. A retired person, a self-employed applicant, and a young adult may need to explain their circumstances differently.

The goal is not to create an artificial picture of strong ties. It is to present a credible and well-supported explanation of the applicant's actual situation.

## How Much Money Do You Need for a Canada Visitor Visa?

There is no single fixed amount that guarantees approval for every visitor visa applicant.

The funds you may need depend on factors such as:

- The length of your stay.
- Whether you will stay with relatives or pay for a hotel.
- The cost of flights and local transportation.
- Your planned activities.
- Whether your host will pay some expenses.
- Your own financial circumstances.

For example, someone staying with a sibling for two weeks may have different accommodation costs from someone planning a month-long trip involving hotels and sightseeing.

Your application should explain who will pay for the visit and provide supporting evidence that fits that arrangement. If a host is paying expenses, the visitor should still describe their own circumstances accurately and submit any evidence required by IRCC.

Avoid borrowing money temporarily or presenting financial documents in a misleading way just to make an application appear stronger. False or misleading information can have serious immigration consequences.

## Common Reasons a Visitor Visa Application May Be Refused

Every application is assessed individually, but concerns can arise when the evidence does not sufficiently establish that the applicant meets the requirements.

Potential issues include:

### Unclear purpose of travel

If the application does not explain why the visitor is travelling, whom they intend to visit, or how long they plan to stay, the officer may have difficulty assessing the purpose of the trip.

### Insufficient financial evidence

The applicant may not have demonstrated how the trip will be funded or whether the available funds are appropriate for the proposed visit.

### Weak evidence of temporary intent

If the application does not adequately address the applicant's circumstances outside Canada, the officer may not be satisfied that they will leave at the end of their authorized stay.

### Inconsistent information

Differences between the application form, invitation letter, employment records, financial documents, and proposed travel dates can raise concerns.

### Missing documents

Failing to provide documents requested by IRCC may delay the application or affect the decision.

### Misrepresentation

False statements, altered documents, or important omissions can have serious consequences. Always provide truthful information, even when a particular circumstance seems difficult to explain.

A strong application is not necessarily the one with the most paperwork. It is the one that presents the relevant facts clearly, consistently, and honestly.

## Should You Apply for a Super Visa Instead?

If you are inviting a parent or grandparent to Canada, it may be worth checking whether the Super Visa is more appropriate than a regular visitor visa.

The Super Visa is designed for eligible parents and grandparents of Canadian citizens, permanent residents, and certain other eligible hosts under the applicable rules. It can allow qualifying visitors to stay for extended periods, subject to the program requirements and the conditions of their authorization.

A regular visitor visa may be more suitable for a shorter visit with family or friends.

The two options have different eligibility criteria and supporting-document requirements. For example, Super Visa applications have specific requirements relating to the host's eligibility and financial support, as well as medical insurance and other conditions.

Do not assume that every parent or grandparent qualifies automatically. Review the official [Super Visa guidance](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/visit-canada/parent-grandparent-super-visa.html) before choosing an application route.

## Practical Tips Before Applying for a Canada Visitor Visa

A little preparation can make the process easier to manage.

**Start early.** Processing times vary, so avoid leaving your application until shortly before an important family event.

**Keep your documents consistent.** Your application, invitation letter, financial evidence, and travel plans should accurately reflect the same circumstances.

**Explain unusual circumstances.** If you are self-employed, retired, travelling with a child, or relying on a host to fund your visit, provide the relevant information requested by IRCC.

**Use genuine documents.** Never alter bank statements, employment letters, invitation letters, or other records.

**Check country-specific instructions.** Additional requirements may apply depending on where you are applying from.

**Avoid guaranteed-approval promises.** No consultant or immigration service can guarantee that a visitor visa will be approved.

**Keep copies of your submission.** Save your application, supporting documents, payment confirmation, and correspondence for future reference.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### 1. Can I visit my family in Canada with a visitor visa?

Yes, eligible applicants may apply for a visitor visa to visit family members in Canada. You must meet the applicable requirements and provide supporting documents appropriate to your circumstances.

### 2. Is an invitation letter compulsory?

An invitation letter may be required or useful depending on the circumstances and the applicable instructions. It is not a guarantee of approval. Check the personalized checklist and official guidance for your application.

### 3. How long can I stay in Canada on a visitor visa?

The visa's validity period is not the same as the period you are authorized to stay during a particular visit. Many visitors are allowed to stay for up to six months, but the border services officer may authorize a different period. Check your entry conditions and any documents issued to you.

### 4. Can my family member in Canada pay for my trip?

A host may offer to cover some or all of the visitor's expenses. The application should explain the arrangement and include relevant evidence where required. The applicant must still meet the applicable visa requirements.

### 5. Can I apply if I have been refused a Canadian visitor visa before?

You may be able to apply again, depending on your circumstances. Read the refusal reasons carefully and address the relevant concerns with accurate evidence before submitting another application. A new application does not guarantee approval.

### 6. Can I work in Canada on a visitor visa?

A visitor visa does not normally authorize you to work in Canada. If you intend to work, check the relevant work permit and immigration requirements before making plans.

### 7. Can I apply for a visitor visa to attend a family wedding?

Yes, attending a family wedding may be a valid reason for a temporary visit. Explain the event, your relationship with the people involved, and the intended travel dates, and provide supporting evidence where appropriate.

### 8. Do I need to book flights before applying?

Check the official application instructions before making travel commitments. It is generally prudent to avoid non-refundable bookings until your visa decision and travel arrangements are sufficiently certain.

### 9. What happens if my visa application is refused?

You should read the refusal letter to understand the reasons for the decision. Depending on the circumstances, you may consider obtaining professional advice, gathering additional evidence, and submitting a new application. A new application should address the issues identified in the refusal rather than simply repeat the previous submission.

### 10. Where can I find the official application instructions?

Visit the Government of Canada's [visitor visa application page](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/visit-canada/apply-visitor-visa.html). It provides the application steps, document instructions, and links to related immigration information.
